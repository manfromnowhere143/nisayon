# Sources and transfer limits

Read 24 November 2026. Download identities are in `sources.json`; original papers
remain in `artifacts/ricci-brain-01/sources`. These are author-reported results,
not independent replications. No novelty survey or priority claim is established.

| Source and sections read | Demonstration and assumptions | Ricci must measure |
|---|---|---|
| [Hartley et al., contact-aided InEKF](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364919894385), author manuscript arXiv:1904.09251v2, §§5–9, Appendix A | Lie-group inertial/contact propagation, kinematic corrections, bias augmentation and contact addition/removal. Cassie uses an 800 Hz IMU, 2 kHz encoders and spring-deflection contact indications. Convergence comparisons reuse logged measurements with randomized initialization; separate motion capture measures position over an approximately 15 m walk. Global position/yaw remain unobservable; bias augmentation loses some state-independent error properties. | Frame/gravity/bias correctness, contact rejection, uncertainty consistency and closed-loop behavior with the proposed transducers. Our multiplicative error-state baseline is not a reproduced InEKF implementation. |
| [Teng et al., slippery-environment InEKF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9561313/), [author PDF](https://hybrid-robotics.berkeley.edu/publications/ICRA2021_InEKF-Cassie-Slip-Estimation.pdf), §§II–V | Cassie slip experiment combines IMU/kinematics with a 200 Hz RealSense T265 velocity signal, models camera misalignment and adapts measurement noise. Motion capture supplies evaluation truth. Velocity observations relax fixed-contact dependence; observability has singular cases. | The camera velocity signal is absent here. Foot/wrist loads plus proprioception cannot be presumed to inherit its slip-estimation performance. Measure observability and late/ambiguous histories directly. |
| [AdaptManip v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14363v1), §§III–V, Tables II–III | G1 uses a learned base locomotion policy, residual manipulation and recurrent object estimation. D435i/AprilTag observations and Mid-360 LiDAR localization complement proprioception. Training uses noisy/masked simulated poses and privileged supervision; policies run at 50 Hz, physics at 200 Hz. Evaluations include IsaacLab-to-MuJoCo transfer, 135 trials per reported simulator comparison and a 50-episode estimator analysis; real lifting/delivery is demonstrated. | No camera, LiDAR, pretrained policy or grasp acquisition is borrowed. Lifting, carrying and recurrent estimation are established prior art. Test whether matched observations change a useful recovery within the available time. |
| [HULK v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08970v1), §§III–V and teacher appendices | Force-aware centroidal MPC guides wrist-force and whole-body-grasp teachers; a capture-point barrier shapes training, then teachers are distilled. G1 comparisons include matched commands/perturbations, five evaluation-seed aggregates, hardware common-load comparisons and separate maximum-load sweeps. Training uses known forces/grasp configurations; whole-body grasps start established. The exported learned controller is unfiltered. | A centroidal target does not establish joint torque feasibility. Test transitions and grasp assumptions explicitly; neither runtime safety guarantees nor general contact-transition competence transfer. |
| [GR00T 2](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/assets/gr00t2_whitepaper.pdf), §3.5, Appendix H.1–H.3, Tables 25–26 | G1 stack separates task selection, Turbo motion generation and SONIC joint targets. Turbo predicts 1.6 s chunks at 1 Hz; SONIC consumes motion tokens at 50 Hz. Compared S1 models share demonstrations, cameras and SONIC. Four hardware task families use binary full-sequence success, including 48 popcorn and 30 trials for each other family. | Preserve the separation with typed task intents, but do not import VLA or learned torque control. Measure our whole-body feasibility, effective latency and task outcomes independently. |
| [Scott et al., Automatica 2014](https://web.mit.edu/braatzgroup/input_design_for_guaranteed_fault_diagnosis_using_zonotopes.pdf), §§2–7, numerical examples §8 | Separating inputs are optimized for a specified finite set of discrete linear models with bounded disturbances and measurement errors. Zonotope propagation gives an MIQP; reductions trade conservatism against complexity. Examples include a second-order system and a low-frequency DC motor with experimentally identified fault parameters. | Future research only. Nonlinear contact dynamics, unknown faults and uncertain sensing do not satisfy these premises by assertion. No active probing or diagnostic guarantee is implemented. |

Model: [MuJoCo Menagerie G1](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/tree/eea4c7431b63da8278b0ff31638e0b0e941cec0e/unitree_g1),
BSD-3-Clause. `reference.json` binds the exact MJCF and meshes. Upstream describes
it as a simplified reference; its position gains require tuning. This experiment
uses direct torque actuators bounded by the same per-joint force limits.

Implementation references: [MuJoCo 3.3.2 dynamics](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.2/computation/index.html),
[state, Jacobian and stepping APIs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.2/APIreference/APIfunctions.html),
and [OSQP Python interface](https://osqp.org/docs/interfaces/python.html). Numerical
solver consistency is a software property, not a physical truth guarantee.
