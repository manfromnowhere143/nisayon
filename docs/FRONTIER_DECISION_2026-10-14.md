## N045: price valid confirmation before expanding it · 16 October 2026

[The completed research and finite qualification](experiments/results/prediction-audit-001/README.md)
adds one conditional option to N041's direction. A prediction available before
execution can inform a randomized audit process whose residual correction keeps

Published prediction-powered e-values already provide this mechanism; it is not
Nisayon's mathematical novelty. A newer bounded-mean Bayes-assisted framework
also supplies finite-time validity under wrong priors, separately from its
asymptotic efficiency result. The older paper's asymptotic scope below remains
historical and should not be generalized to this newer result.

The constructed perfect-predictor comparison uses 22.105 expected acquisitions
versus 26.840 for a competent sequential test, after adding early futility to
both. Support probabilities are 85.748% and 85.435%. All 118 eligible fixed
binomial rules were also checked; the best costs 27.584. Only 4.735 execution
cost units remain to pay for extra contexts, predictions, preparation, review,
adoption and maintenance. Even with other extra costs zero, common context cost
must be below 27.26% of avoidable execution cost. Under the null the audit
method costs more. An always-positive predictor has 95% accuracy at the planning
alternative yet fails the planned support target. There is no uniform saving.

The owner intake should therefore inspect when a candidate signal becomes
available and what acquiring it costs. A score computed after the full rollout
does not save that rollout. Omitted executions must preserve the target sampling
and reset process. Reuse may amortize checked preparation, but its complete
cost is still unknown. The existing 32-pair finite obligation cannot be shortened
by substituting a population-mean test. No new production rule, predictor,
policy, simulator or hardware execution was added. Pending engagements were
preserved without polling or new messages. Owner evidence and separate untouched

**Decision: refine the reusable-experiment hypothesis around the owner's

[EmbodiedRSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10498v1) already describes competing
hypotheses, information gain per experiment cost and reusable memory.
[EmbodiedEvo](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18867v2) retains skills and lessons for
fresh test workspaces. [ENPIRE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19980v2) runs physical

coding loop or memory store is insufficient differentiation.

### The decision that matters

The owner needs to choose a correction, retain the current deployment, or leave
the issue unresolved. Identifying every possible cause can cost more without
changing that choice. When several explanations imply the same correction, an
experiment that only separates their labels has no immediate decision value.
Conversely, a cheap check that rules out a costly, ineffective correction can
matter even when the full cause remains uncertain. This is standard decision
theory applied to deployment work, not a new mathematical claim.

Use N039's finite mechanism only when its distinctions affect available remedies
or the validity of confirmation. Begin with the actual deployment change,
candidate remedies, unresolved evidence and strongest native checks. Select the
smallest valid experiment that could alter the choice. Any experiment still
needs accurate state, history, timing, reset and outcome semantics; information
gain cannot repair an invalid intervention.

When a known working deployment exists, use it to constrain the question.
Bind the working and changed versions, checkpoint, transforms and execution
state before choosing an intervention. A small deployment difference can give
a much narrower experiment than open-ended robot improvement. The old success
does not prove today's cause or supply a counterfactual trajectory; it supplies
a reference whose relevance can be checked. Prefer such incidents for intake.

The proposed reusable unit is the competing corrections, their distinguishing
test, observed intervention outcomes, applicability conditions and regression.
Reuse the checked preparation and procedure where those conditions match.
Fresh target task confirmation remains necessary. A later incident may benefit
from prior knowledge without sharing the prior confirmation data. Any reusable
owner evidence must stay within the owner's permitted scope.

### The proposed source of compounding value

An ambiguous trace cannot justify choosing one explanation merely because it
has a familiar label. Half gain and one-step delay can produce identical
observations on a doubling input. Holding the input separates them. Retaining
that input, competing predictions, error bound and reset conditions makes the
experiment available for a later compatible incident.

The product hypothesis is that a growing set of applicable, checked experiments
reduces repeated engineering and wrong correction attempts before expensive
confirmation. This is an inference about possible value, not a demonstrated
moat. The mathematics is standard finite identification and set cover. Its
potential value would come from reliable case preparation, reuse and lower
complete decision cost. A library that costs more to maintain than it saves
would falsify this direction.

## Mathematics that earns its place

For a declared task population and execution regime, the target is the change
in task outcome, not prediction accuracy:

\[
\Delta = \mathbb{E}[Y(\text{correction})-Y(\text{unchanged})].
\]

The regime includes model version, observation/action transforms, history and
reset rules, controller and timing. Restoring a simulator snapshot can support
paired branching only when the relevant execution state is also restored.
Physical trials require a defensible assignment and reset protocol. Reusing
recorded future observations after changing actions cannot estimate this effect.

The 2026 [active prediction-powered inference study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18569v1)
examines residual-corrected estimates of the form

\[
\widehat\mu = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[f_t(X_t)+\frac{I_t}{p_t(X_t)}(Y_t-f_t(X_t))\right].
\]

Its fixed-population, predictable-query setting needs positive sampling
probabilities and the corresponding conditional query randomization. Its
experiments caution against assuming learned uncertainty sampling beats uniform
sampling; its context-oblivious result is not a theorem against all active
selection. For Nisayon, reducing labels is distinct from reducing robot trials:
if obtaining the predictor's inputs already requires the rollout, that execution
cost has already been paid. Missing outcomes with unknown selection probabilities
cannot simply be repaired by this formula.

The [Bayes-assisted PPI paper, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18000v2) has released
[code](https://github.com/stefanocortinovis/ppi-cs/tree/20f1cd0364b054e781a74e64dac2782bb2dff337),
but its general confidence sequences are **asymptotic**. Do not turn that into
a finite-sample certificate for a few robot trials. Use a fixed confirmation
design first; if optional stopping is useful, select a
[nonasymptotic confidence sequence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240v9) whose
assumptions fit the sampling unit and process. Account for repeated looks,
multiple candidates and required groups. No new statistical selector is allocated.

## The milestone and the experiment that can reject it

**Milestone: one independently owned repair decision, followed by a separate
untouched incident.** Use the owner's existing deployment and native tools.

1. Bind the deployed source, settings, outcomes and live choice. Identify which
   observation would distinguish runtime mismatch from policy insufficiency.
   Inspect conventional source/configuration explanations first. Initial intake
   remains one incident and at most four engineering hours.
2. Freeze the outcome predicate, target population, reset/history rules,
   sampling unit, quality margin, stopping rule and confirmation budget before
   new outcomes. Any later task execution needs that concrete allocation.
3. Compare a capable engineer/agent using native tools with the same workflow
   plus the smallest decision-relevant Nisayon check. Give both equal starting
   information; preserve separation so the first solver does not teach the
   second the answer. Confirm proposed corrections on fresh conditions with
   an unchanged control and appropriate assignment order.
4. Count setup, diagnosis, failed attempts, physical trials, adoption, review and
   maintenance. Report labor, elapsed time, compute and robot use separately;
   combine them only with agreed weights. Unknown costs remain unknown.

Accept a first value case only when the correction meets the frozen quality
rule and Nisayon lowers complete work under the owner's cost measure. Overlapping
cost uncertainty, unavailable outcomes or invalid pairing mean unresolved.
Equal or greater cost for the same accepted answer rejects the value claim on
that case; loss of benefit on fresh conditions rejects the correction. Neither
result should be explained away by adding a selector. The historical twofold
cost target remains an ambition, not a measured result or newly opened allocation.

Repeat use must then show that the integration is reusable. A growing collection
of permitted intervention/outcome cases could improve future experiment choices
